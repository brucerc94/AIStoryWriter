Generate a detailed, complete chapter-by-chapter outline for this novel.

The author's explicit story request and the Synopsis are the authoritative source material. Preserve every concrete event, action, outcome, consequence, relationship change, setting change, revelation, decision, and sequence they establish.

The outline is the binding narrative blueprint for the chapter writer. Its purpose is to provide enough concrete information that the chapter writer can develop the story into full literary scenes without having to invent the core events, character behavior, important interactions, or major transitions.

DEPTH REQUIREMENT

Make the outline as detailed and information-dense as the story requires. Do NOT compress the story into a high-level summary merely because it is called an outline. For every chapter, describe the complete progression of the chapter from opening state to ending state.

For each chapter, explicitly include whenever relevant:
- where and when the chapter begins;
- which established characters are present and what each is doing;
- the important physical actions and reactions of each relevant character;
- the sequence and causal relationship of major events;
- important interactions between characters;
- important dialogue content, questions, answers, statements, arguments, revelations, promises, refusals, or decisions that the chapter must contain;
- discoveries and exactly what information becomes known, by whom, and when;
- conflicts, obstacles, attempts, responses, and consequences;
- emotional or psychological changes that materially affect the story;
- relationship changes and what causes them;
- relevant setting details needed for the scene or story logic;
- important objects, symbols, powers, rules, or resources involved in the events;
- transitions between locations, scenes, time periods, or points of view when needed;
- what remains unresolved;
- the exact narrative situation at the end of the chapter.

Treat the Synopsis as a full source of story material. When the Synopsis already specifies a concrete event, preserve it explicitly in the appropriate chapter instead of replacing it with a vague summary.

Do not merely repeat the Synopsis chapter by chapter. Distribute its events deliberately across the requested chapters and add useful connective detail so that every chapter has a concrete beginning, progression, and ending.

Do not leave core events for the chapter writer to invent. Avoid vague phrases such as "a conflict occurs", "they discuss the issue", "something happens", "the situation changes", or "they have an emotional conversation" when the underlying event is known from the Synopsis or established story context. State the actual event.

Do NOT turn the outline into finished literary prose. Use factual, direct, information-dense planning language. The chapter writer will later apply the requested literary style, scene writing, sensory detail, dialogue prose, pacing, and emotional texture.

SOURCE FIDELITY / NO INVENTED CANON

Do not invent facts merely to make the outline sound more detailed.

In particular, do NOT invent:
- new named characters that are not required by the story;
- arbitrary names for unnamed children or minor people unless a genuinely new named character is necessary;
- exact quotations that were not provided by the author or already established in the story;
- exact measurements, temperatures, times, distances, quantities, or numerical values not established by the source material;
- new magical rules, powers, objects, locations, causes, mechanisms, diagnoses, secrets, motives, or backstory not supported by the source material;
- new plot twists, revelations, relationships, deaths, injuries, resolutions, betrayals, or major events not supported by the source material;
- new causal explanations that change the meaning of the author's story.

You may add connective scene details only when they are a natural elaboration of an already established event and do not create new canon. Prefer neutral phrasing over invented specifics when the source does not define the detail.

Do not convert an inference into a hard fact unless the source supports that inference.

CHARACTERS AND CONTINUITY

Use exact established character names whenever they exist. Do not use a title, epithet, pronoun, profession, physical description, or role as though it were a separate character.

When multiple characters are present, attribute actions, decisions, discoveries, and reactions to the correct named character. Avoid ambiguous pronouns when the referent could be unclear.

Use only established characters unless a genuinely new character is necessary for the story. Respect established relationships, character identities, world rules, and Memory exactly. Do not contradict established continuity.

If a chapter changes a relationship, state the cause and resulting state explicitly.

CHAPTER STRUCTURE

For each chapter, use exactly this structure and no additional top-level chapter sections:

## Chapter N: Title

Chapter Plan:
A detailed, chronological account of the chapter. Include the chapter opening state, location/time, characters present, concrete actions, important interactions and dialogue content, discoveries, decisions, conflicts, reactions, consequences, emotional developments, relationship changes, relevant setting information, transitions between major events, unresolved issues, and the exact situation at the end of the chapter. Include all important concrete story beats required by the Synopsis and established story context. The Chapter Plan should be detailed enough that the writer can turn it into complete scenes without inventing the chapter's core events.

Continuity:
State the concrete story state established by the end of the chapter and required for later chapters. Include relevant character states, relationship states, information known by specific characters, important objects or statuses, locations, active consequences, unresolved problems, and any event, revelation, relationship change, or outcome that must not happen yet.

Do not duplicate the entire Chapter Plan inside Continuity. Continuity records the resulting state and constraints that matter after the chapter ends.

FORMAT RULES

Return ONLY the complete outline. Do NOT add:
- an introduction;
- a preface;
- an explanation of how you interpreted the request;
- a note about the requested chapter count;
- a disclaimer;
- a conclusion after the final chapter;
- meta-commentary about the outline.

Do not add sections such as "Characters Present", "Beginning", "Sequence of Events", "Rising Action", "Climax", "Resolution", "Summary", "Notes", "Focus", "Objective", "Story Progression", "Restrictions", or any other extra section inside a chapter. Put that information directly inside Chapter Plan or Continuity.

Do not wrap the outline in Markdown code fences.

Each chapter entry ends after its Continuity section. Do not append any text after the final chapter.

The chapter title may be descriptive, but do not insert information into the title that changes the story's facts.

The requested chapter count is a planning constraint, not a reason to reduce detail.
