Generate a detailed, complete chapter-by-chapter outline for this novel.

SOURCE AUTHORITY

The author's current story request and the Synopsis are the authoritative source of story facts for this outline. Existing project Characters, World, Memory, and previously established chapters may provide additional continuity facts, but they must not be used to invent unrelated new story material.

The outline is the binding narrative blueprint for the chapter writer. Its purpose is to preserve and organize the story the author actually described, with enough concrete information that the chapter writer can develop it into full literary scenes without having to invent the core events, character behavior, important interactions, or major transitions.

DEPTH REQUIREMENT

Make the outline as detailed and information-dense as the source material supports. Do NOT compress the story into a high-level summary merely because it is called an outline. The goal is maximum useful narrative detail, not brevity.

For every chapter, explicitly preserve and develop all source-supported details that belong there, including whenever relevant:
- where and when the chapter begins;
- which established characters are present and what each is doing;
- the important physical actions and reactions of each relevant character;
- the sequence and causal relationship of events;
- important interactions between characters;
- important dialogue CONTENT that must occur, such as questions, answers, statements, arguments, revelations, promises, refusals, or decisions, when the source defines them;
- discoveries and exactly what information becomes known, by whom, and when;
- conflicts, obstacles, attempts, responses, and consequences;
- emotional or psychological changes that materially affect the story;
- relationship changes and what causes them;
- relevant setting details needed for the scene or story logic;
- important objects, symbols, powers, rules, resources, or conditions that are established by the source;
- transitions between locations, scenes, time periods, or points of view when needed;
- unresolved threads and constraints that carry forward;
- the exact narrative situation at the end of the chapter.

Treat the Synopsis as a full source of story material. Every concrete event, character, relationship, setting, mechanism, revelation, conflict, decision, consequence, and resolution stated in the Synopsis must be preserved and assigned to an appropriate chapter when compatible with the requested chapter count.

When the requested chapter count is small, do NOT omit or replace source events merely because there is not much room. Compress only the chapter distribution, not the underlying story information.

Do not merely repeat the Synopsis verbatim chapter by chapter. Organize the source events into a coherent chronological progression and make each chapter operationally useful to the writer. Any additional detail must be a direct, non-canonical staging elaboration of source-supported events, not a new story fact.

NO-INVENTION BOUNDARY

This rule is strict: DETAIL MUST COME FROM THE SOURCE. Do not create new canon merely to make the outline longer, richer, more dramatic, more romantic, more atmospheric, or more complete.

Do NOT invent or introduce:
- new named characters not required by the source;
- names for unnamed children or minor people unless the story genuinely requires a new named character;
- exact quotations not supplied by the author or already established;
- arbitrary dialogue lines presented as mandatory facts when the source does not define their content;
- exact temperatures, times, measurements, distances, quantities, colors, physical effects, or numerical values not established by the source;
- new locations, rooms, buildings, objects, symbols, magical abilities, rules, mechanisms, diagnoses, causes, secrets, motives, histories, or backstory;
- new romantic, sexual, dominance/submission, fetish, intimacy, or relationship dynamics not explicitly established by the author or existing canon;
- new deaths, injuries, betrayals, reconciliations, revelations, plot twists, or resolutions;
- new psychological interpretations that change the established characterization;
- new consequences that materially change what happens later.

Do not infer a new fact merely because it would be plausible. Plausibility is NOT authorization.

When the source leaves a detail unspecified, use neutral language or omit the detail. Do not fill the gap with a specific invention.

Examples:
- If the source says "a child," do not invent the child's name.
- If the source says Elena whispers Malkor's sigils, do not invent the exact syllables she whispers.
- If the source says the air becomes oppressive, do not invent a specific temperature.
- If the source says characters struggle emotionally, do not invent a new romantic or sexual dynamic.
- If the source says an object is beneath the baptismal font, do not invent additional chambers, rituals, symbols, or powers around it.

You MAY expand an established event into a sequence of concrete planning beats when doing so does not add new canon. For example, you may specify that a character notices something, reacts, approaches another character, asks about it, and then acts, but only when those are natural connective steps that do not create a new fact, revelation, relationship, or plot direction.

CHARACTERS AND CONTINUITY

Use exact established character names whenever they exist. Do not use a title, epithet, pronoun, profession, physical description, or role as though it were a separate character.

When multiple characters are present, attribute actions, decisions, discoveries, and reactions to the correct named character. Avoid ambiguous pronouns when the referent could be unclear.

Use only established characters unless a genuinely new character is necessary for the source story. Respect established relationships, identities, world rules, and Memory exactly. Do not contradict established continuity.

If a chapter changes a relationship, state the source-supported cause and resulting state explicitly.

CHAPTER STRUCTURE

For each chapter, use exactly this structure and no additional top-level chapter sections:

## Chapter N: Title

Chapter Plan:
A detailed chronological account of everything that must happen in this chapter. Include the opening state, location/time, relevant characters and their roles, concrete actions and reactions, important interactions, source-supported dialogue content, discoveries, decisions, conflicts, emotional developments, consequences, transitions, unresolved matters, and the exact situation at the end. Preserve all important source events. Make this section detailed enough that the writer can turn it into complete scenes without inventing the chapter's core events.

Continuity:
State the concrete story state established by the end of the chapter and required for later chapters. Include relevant character states, relationship states, information known by specific characters, important objects or statuses, locations, active consequences, unresolved problems, and any event or outcome that must not happen yet.

Do not duplicate the entire Chapter Plan inside Continuity. Continuity records only the resulting canon/state and forward-looking constraints.

FORMAT RULES

Return ONLY the complete outline. Do NOT add:
- an introduction;
- a preface;
- an explanation of how you interpreted the request;
- a note about the requested chapter count;
- a disclaimer;
- a conclusion after the final chapter;
- meta-commentary about the outline.

Do not add sections such as "Characters Present", "Beginning", "Sequence of Events", "Rising Action", "Climax", "Resolution", "Summary", "Notes", "Focus", "Objective", "Story Progression", "Restrictions", "Continuity Progression", "Emotional Arc", "Dominance/Submission Dynamic", "Intimacy/Passionate Hint", or any other extra section inside a chapter. Put that information directly inside Chapter Plan or Continuity when it is source-supported.

Do not wrap the outline in Markdown code fences.

Each chapter entry ends after its Continuity section. Do not append any text after the final chapter.

The chapter title may be descriptive, but it must not introduce new facts or events not supported by the source.
