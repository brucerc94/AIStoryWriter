Generate a detailed, complete chapter-by-chapter outline for this novel.

SOURCE AUTHORITY

The author's current story request and Synopsis are the authoritative source of story facts for this outline. Existing project Characters, World, Memory, and previously established chapters may provide additional continuity facts, but they must not be used to invent unrelated new story material.

The outline is the binding narrative blueprint for the chapter writer. Its job is to resolve the story into concrete, ordered, writable events so the chapter writer can focus on prose rather than deciding what happens.

DEPTH REQUIREMENT

Make every Chapter Plan substantially more detailed than a high-level summary. The outline must function as a scene-and-beat blueprint, not as a compressed retelling of the Synopsis.

For every major source event, break it into the smallest useful sequence of concrete narrative beats needed to write the scene. A beat should normally specify, when the source supports it:
- the current scene, location, and relevant time progression;
- the established characters participating and their role in the moment;
- the concrete action or event that occurs;
- the direct response from another character or the environment;
- what is said, communicated, requested, refused, decided, or revealed when the source defines that information;
- the immediate physical, emotional, relational, or plot consequence;
- the transition or causal link into the next beat;
- what important information becomes established by the end of the beat.

Do not be afraid to use many beats. Prefer 8–20+ concrete beats for a chapter when the source contains enough material to support them. Use more when necessary for complex chapters. Do not pad the outline with decorative prose or empty detail merely to increase length.

The goal is MAXIMUM USEFUL DETAIL, not maximum word count.

A Chapter Plan should be sufficiently explicit that the chapter writer does not need to invent any core event, scene purpose, character action, important interaction, discovery, decision, conflict escalation, turning point, or consequence.

SOURCE-EVENT COVERAGE

Map every meaningful story event from the Synopsis into the appropriate chapter. Preserve its order, causality, and consequence.

If the Synopsis contains a sequence such as:
A notices X → A confronts B → B reacts → C intervenes → A learns Y → A makes decision Z
then the outline must preserve those individual beats rather than compressing the sequence into "the conflict escalates" or "A learns what to do."

Expand the source into concrete beats, but do not create new canon while doing so.

If the requested chapter count is small, compress the DISTRIBUTION across chapters, not the underlying story information. The story may be dense inside a chapter. Do not discard meaningful source events merely because there are few chapters.

RESOLVE THE BEAT — DO NOT OFFER OPTIONS

Every important outline beat must be written as a decided event, not as a possibility.

NEVER use speculative planning language such as:
- likely
- perhaps
- maybe
- might
- could
- e.g.
- for example, when presenting the actual event
- "or"
- "such as" when it leaves the event undecided
- placeholders like "a child", "someone", or "an object" when the source already provides the identity

Do not give the chapter writer alternatives to choose from.

Bad:
"Raul is likely painting or supervising the toddlers."

Bad:
"He perhaps consults an old sketch or digs beneath the font."

Good:
"Raul paints with the toddlers while remaining alert to the abnormal gloom outside."

Good:
"Raul examines the area beneath the baptismal font and searches for the physical source of the disturbance."

When the source itself leaves a detail unspecified, keep the detail neutral rather than inventing a specific fact. Do not replace an unknown detail with multiple possibilities.

BEAT GRANULARITY

A useful beat should contain a concrete event and its immediate consequence. When a source event is important or complex, separate its internal sequence into multiple beats instead of putting everything into one vague paragraph.

For example, instead of:
"Raul discovers Elena is affected and becomes worried."

prefer:
"Raul notices Elena separated from the other children and focused on the chipped wooden block. He watches her trace the damaged edge with her fingers."

"Elena begins whispering fragments associated with Malkor's dark sigils while continuing to stare at the block. Raul moves closer to hear her."

"Raul recognizes the significance of what Elena is whispering. His earlier explanation of the village's condition as seasonal no longer fits the evidence, and his concern becomes active suspicion."

The point is not to write finished prose. The point is to specify exactly what the prose must portray.

NO-INVENTION BOUNDARY

DETAIL MUST COME FROM ESTABLISHED STORY MATERIAL.

Do NOT invent:
- new named characters when the source does not require them;
- names for unnamed children or minor people;
- exact quotations unless supplied by the author or already established;
- arbitrary dialogue presented as mandatory facts when the source does not define its content;
- exact temperatures, times, distances, quantities, measurements, or numerical values not established by the source;
- new places, rooms, buildings, objects, symbols, powers, rules, mechanisms, causes, secrets, motives, histories, or backstory;
- new romance, sexual, dominance/submission, fetish, intimacy, or relationship dynamics;
- new deaths, injuries, betrayals, reconciliations, revelations, twists, or resolutions;
- new psychological interpretations that materially change a character;
- new consequences that alter later story events.

Plausibility is not authorization.

CONNECTIVE ACTION

You MAY add ordinary connective actions needed to make an established event writable, but only when they do not add new canon.

Safe connective actions include:
- a character approaches another character;
- a character looks, notices, listens, asks, answers, pauses, or moves between already-established actions;
- a character physically transitions from one already-established location to another;
- a character reacts to an established event;
- a scene transitions naturally to the next established event.

Unsafe connective additions are anything that create new facts, motives, relationships, revelations, named objects, secrets, mechanisms, or plot directions.

CHARACTERS AND WORLD

Use exact established character names whenever they exist. Do not treat titles, professions, epithets, pronouns, or descriptions as separate characters.

Existing Characters and World are authoritative reference material for established canon and constraints. Use them to make the Chapter Plan concrete and consistent with known character behavior, relationships, setting rules, and world facts.

The Outline may describe what an established character does, says, knows, notices, decides, or feels when the source and existing canon support it.

When a World rule or setting fact affects an event, put that relevant information directly into the Chapter Plan so the chapter writer knows how the scene should work. Do not copy the entire World document into every chapter.

Do not invent new worldbuilding simply because a World section exists.

CONTINUITY

For each chapter, the Continuity section records ONLY the state established after the chapter.

Include, when relevant:
- character state changes;
- relationship changes;
- information known by specific characters;
- important object/status changes;
- current locations when narratively relevant;
- active consequences;
- unresolved plot threads;
- explicit constraints on what has NOT happened yet or is still unknown.

Continuity is a state record, not a second Chapter Plan. Do not duplicate the chapter's event sequence here.

STRUCTURE

Inside Chapter Plan, organize the material chronologically.

Use a numbered list of narrative beats when it improves clarity, but do not create extra Markdown headings inside a chapter.

Each numbered beat should be a concrete instruction to the writer. Multiple sentences are encouraged when needed to resolve the event fully.

FORMAT

Return ONLY the outline.

Use exactly this structure for every chapter and no other chapter-level sections:

## Chapter N: Title

Chapter Plan:
[detailed chronological planning beats]

Continuity:
[resulting canon/state]

Do NOT add any additional headings or sections such as:
- Characters Present
- Beginning
- Middle
- Rising Action
- Sequence of Events
- Key Events
- Detailed Action Progression
- Climax
- Resolution
- Summary
- Notes
- Focus
- Objective
- Story Progression
- Restrictions
- Continuity Progression
- Emotional Arc
- Dominance/Submission Dynamic
- Intimacy/Passionate Hint
- Theme
- Ending
- or any other extra section.

All such information belongs inside Chapter Plan or Continuity when relevant.

Do not add an introduction, preface, explanation, disclaimer, requested-count note, or conclusion after the final chapter.
Do not wrap the outline in code fences.
