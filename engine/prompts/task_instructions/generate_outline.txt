Generate a detailed, complete chapter-by-chapter outline for this novel.

SOURCE AUTHORITY

The author's current story request and Synopsis are the authoritative source of story facts for this outline. Existing project Characters, World, Memory, and previously established chapters may provide additional continuity facts, but they must not be used to invent unrelated new story material.

The outline is the binding narrative blueprint for the chapter writer. Its job is to tell the writer, in concrete terms, what each chapter needs to do. A strong outline is not a synopsis repeated with different wording; it is a detailed operational plan for the scenes and beats that will later become prose.

DEPTH REQUIREMENT

Make every Chapter Plan substantially more detailed than a high-level summary. Use the source material to expand each major event into a sequence of concrete narrative beats.

For each major event from the Synopsis, develop as many source-supported beats as are useful. A beat may describe:
- the current scene/location/time;
- which established characters are present and their immediate purpose;
- the concrete action that starts the beat;
- what another character notices, says, asks, refuses, decides, or does in response;
- important dialogue CONTENT when the source defines what must be communicated;
- the immediate consequence of the action;
- how the story moves into the next beat;
- what the reader should understand by the end of the beat;
- the resulting state before the next beat begins.

Do not be afraid to use many beats. The goal is maximum useful detail, not brevity. If the Synopsis gives a sequence of five meaningful actions, the outline should preserve all five rather than compressing them into one sentence such as "the conflict escalates."

A Chapter Plan should make the chapter writable without requiring the chapter writer to invent the core events, character behavior, important interactions, discoveries, decisions, or major transitions.

Every concrete story element in the Synopsis that belongs in a chapter must remain visible somewhere in that chapter's Chapter Plan. Preserve the sequence and causal relationships between events. When the source provides a beginning, escalation, turning point, climax, resolution, or consequence, retain those elements when they belong to the requested chapter distribution.

If the requested chapter count is small, compress the DISTRIBUTION across chapters, not the underlying story information. Do not replace several source events with a generic summary just because the chapter count is small.

STRUCTURE OF EACH CHAPTER PLAN

Inside Chapter Plan, organize the material chronologically. You may use a numbered list of narrative beats, but do not create extra Markdown sections/headings inside the chapter.

A useful beat should read like an instruction to the writer, for example:
"Raul notices Elena staring at the chipped wooden block. He approaches her and asks what she is whispering. Elena does not answer and continues tracing the block with her fingers. Raul recognizes that her behavior is abnormal and begins to question his belief that the village's condition is merely seasonal."

This is preferred over:
"Raul discovers something is wrong with Elena."

The detailed version must still remain faithful to established facts. It may expand connective actions and reactions, but it must not create new canon.

NO-INVENTION BOUNDARY

DETAIL MUST COME FROM THE SOURCE.

Do NOT invent:
- new named characters when the source does not require them;
- names for unnamed children or minor people;
- exact quotations unless supplied by the author or already established;
- arbitrary dialogue presented as mandatory facts when the source does not define its content;
- exact temperatures, times, distances, quantities, measurements, or numerical values not established by the source;
- new places, rooms, buildings, objects, symbols, powers, rules, mechanisms, causes, secrets, motives, histories, or backstory;
- new romance, sexual, dominance/submission, fetish, intimacy, or relationship dynamics;
- new deaths, injuries, betrayals, reconciliations, revelations, twists, or resolutions;
- new psychological interpretations that materially change a character;
- new consequences that alter later story events.

If a detail is unspecified, keep it neutral or omit it. Plausibility is not authorization.

You MAY expand source-supported events into natural connective beats. For example: noticing something, approaching another character, asking a question, receiving a response, reacting, and then acting can be used to make an established event writable, provided those connective steps do not create a new fact, revelation, relationship, or plot direction.

CHARACTERS AND WORLD

Use exact established character names whenever they exist. Do not treat titles, professions, epithets, pronouns, or descriptions as separate characters.

Existing Characters and World are reference material for the outline. Use them when they clarify established facts, relationships, setting rules, or continuity. Do not override the Synopsis with them unless they contain already-established canon that must be preserved.

When a character is relevant to a chapter, make the Chapter Plan explicit about what that established character is doing, how they respond, what they know, and what changes for them, when the source supports those details.

When a setting or world rule is relevant to an event, incorporate the established World detail into the Chapter Plan so the chapter writer knows how the scene should work. Do not invent additional worldbuilding merely because World material is available.

CONTINUITY

For each chapter, the Continuity section records only the resulting state that must be true after the chapter:
- character state changes;
- relationship changes;
- information known by specific characters;
- important object/status changes;
- current locations when narratively relevant;
- active consequences;
- unresolved threads;
- explicit constraints on what has NOT happened yet.

Do not duplicate the whole Chapter Plan in Continuity.

FORMAT

Return ONLY the outline.

Use exactly this structure for every chapter and no other chapter-level sections:

## Chapter N: Title

Chapter Plan:
[detailed chronological planning beats]

Continuity:
[resulting canon/state]

Do NOT add any additional headings or sections such as:
- Characters Present
- Beginning
- Middle
- Rising Action
- Sequence of Events
- Key Events
- Detailed Action Progression
- Climax
- Resolution
- Summary
- Notes
- Focus
- Objective
- Story Progression
- Restrictions
- Continuity Progression
- Emotional Arc
- Dominance/Submission Dynamic
- Intimacy/Passionate Hint
- Theme
- Ending
- or any other extra section.

All such information belongs inside Chapter Plan or Continuity when relevant.

Do not add an introduction, preface, explanation, disclaimer, requested-count note, or conclusion after the final chapter.
Do not wrap the outline in code fences.
