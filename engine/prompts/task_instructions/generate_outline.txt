Generate a detailed chapter-by-chapter outline for this novel.

The author's explicit story request is authoritative. Preserve every concrete event, action, outcome, consequence, relationship change, setting change, and sequence that the author requests.

Do not soften, euphemize, generalize, omit, replace, or reinterpret explicit story events. If the author specifies that a character kills someone, dies, is injured, uses violence, causes destruction, reveals a secret, or performs any other concrete action, the outline must state that event clearly and accurately. Do not replace a concrete event with vague wording such as "a violent confrontation", "a serious incident", or "a major conflict".

The outline is the binding narrative blueprint for the chapter writer. It must specify the concrete story events that the chapter writer is required to depict, not merely the chapter's theme, purpose, or a high-level summary.

Plan the complete story first, then distribute it across the requested chapters. For every chapter, explicitly define what happens and in what order. Include the characters who are present, where the chapter begins, their concrete actions, important interactions and dialogue content that must occur when relevant, discoveries, decisions, conflicts, reactions, consequences, setting changes, relationship changes, transitions between important events, and the exact situation in which the chapter ends.

A chapter entry must contain enough concrete narrative information that the chapter writer can write the chapter accurately without having to invent the missing core events. Do not use placeholders such as "something happens", "they discuss the issue", "a conflict occurs", or "the situation changes" when the underlying event can be stated directly.

Use the author's requested writing style and level of detail as a planning-depth constraint. If the author's style requests detailed, rich, immersive, highly descriptive, emotionally deep, highly visual, or otherwise elaborate writing, increase the amount of useful concrete narrative information in the chapter plan accordingly. Add more relevant story beats, character actions, interactions, reactions, discoveries, decisions, consequences, setting information, relationship developments, and transitions rather than merely making the wording longer or more elaborate. The goal is to give the chapter writer a rich factual blueprint that supports the requested level of narrative depth.

Do not turn the chapter plan into finished literary prose. Do not add stylistic flourishes, atmospheric filler, sensory embellishment, or polished dialogue merely to imitate the final writing style. Style controls the depth and richness of the underlying plan; the chapter writer later applies the literary style to that plan.

When two or more characters appear in the same chapter, refer to them by their exact established names whenever there is any possibility of ambiguity about who is doing what. Do not rely on pronouns such as "he", "she", "they", "him", "her", or "them" if more than one character could be the referent, or if the referent could otherwise be unclear to a reader. Every action, decision, and discovery must be attributed unambiguously to the correct named character.

Never turn a pronoun, title, epithet, or physical description into a new character. Only write actions for established named characters, or for a genuinely new character the story requires. Do not invent a separate character out of a description like "the traveler" or "the old man" unless that description is the character's established name.

Use only established characters unless introducing a new one is genuinely necessary. Respect Character Relationships, World, and Memory exactly as given, and do not contradict established continuity.

For each chapter, use exactly this structure:

## Chapter N: Title

Chapter Plan:
A detailed, connected account of everything that must happen in this chapter, in chronological order. State where the chapter begins, which named characters are present, what each character does, the important interactions and dialogue content that must occur when relevant, what information is revealed or discovered, what decisions are made, what conflicts or actions occur, what reactions and consequences follow, how relationships or the situation change, what relevant setting details must be established, how the chapter transitions between major events, and the exact state in which the chapter ends. Include enough concrete story beats that the chapter writer does not need to invent the chapter's core events. When the author's style requests high detail, increase the amount of concrete narrative information rather than repeating the same ideas with more words.

Continuity:
State the concrete story state established by the end of the chapter and required for later chapters. Include character states, relationship states, new information known by specific characters, important objects, locations, unresolved problems, consequences that are now in effect, and any event, revelation, relationship change, or outcome that must not happen yet.

The chapter plan is a planning document, not finished prose. Keep it factual, direct, and information-dense. Do not duplicate the same information across multiple sections. Do not summarize events that can instead be stated concretely. The Chapter Plan should contain the narrative information; Continuity should contain only the resulting state and constraints that matter after the chapter ends.
